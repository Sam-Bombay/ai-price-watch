The assumptions, in full

One task = turns. Each turn resends a -token base (system prompt and tool schemas), plus everything produced so far: output tokens and tokens of tool result per turn. That growth is what makes the curve quadratic, and it is the part missing from every per-token comparison.

These are stated assumptions, not measurements of your traffic. Change the turn count and the ranking stays, the dollar figures move. Tier matters more than price: a small model is not a like-for-like swap for a frontier one on a hard task, so no same-tier swap here exceeds a x cut.

Why this is the number that gets missed

A CFO approves "an agent" against a per-token rate. The bill arrives after the agent has run a few thousand tasks, each one quietly re-reading its own context. The same 20-turn task costs on the cheapest model in this panel and on the most expensive: the work is identical, the price of doing it is not.

That gap is what an audit finds. What an audit costs and what it covers - fixed price, refunded if it does not find at least 15%.
