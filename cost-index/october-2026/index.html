What the index measures

One workload, held constant: 120 million input tokens and 18 million output tokens a month — a 40-person company running one product feature on a model. That workload is priced every day against the same set of models, so the index measures what happened to the price of a fixed capability, not what happened to the market's average.

Models that entered or left the catalogue during the window are excluded from the panel and reported separately. That is deliberate: new models are a different effect, and mixing them in is how a price index ends up measuring product churn instead of price.

Why this is the number that matters

The cheap tier did not get cheaper. The cheapest capable route for this workload rose 56% in three weeks, and the 90th percentile rose 24%. A budget set in September on "we'll just use the cheap model" is now wrong by half.

That is the whole argument for measuring rather than assuming. The market's headline story in October was a price war; the fixed-panel data says the same work got more expensive, and the budget tier got more expensive fastest.

Method and audit trail

Prices are read from the public OpenRouter catalogue once a day. Every change is logged with a date, the model id, the field, and the before/after value. The index is rebuilt by walking backwards from today's prices and reversing every logged event dated after each day — so each day's figure is the catalogue exactly as the scraper saw it, not an interpolation.
