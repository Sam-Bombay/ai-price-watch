The same day, one model halved and another went up 19 times — 27 Oct 2026

OpenAI cut the old Terra to the price of the new one, and DeepSeek tripled down on its floor. 42 changes logged today across 463 models: 31 price moves (16 input, 15 output), 4 new models, 7 retirements — 14 cuts, 17 rises. OpenAI shipped GPT-6.1 Terra (plus Pro and batch variants) at $1 / $5 and cut GPT-5.6 Terra and the GPT Terra Latest alias to the identical rate — input $2.00 → $1.00 (-50.0%), output $12.00 → $5.00 (-58.3%). The batch door on the same model is $0.50 / $2.50.

In the other direction, the cheap floor went vertical. DeepSeek Pro Latest output $0.396 → $8.00 (+1,920%) — twenty times, in one day. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash input $0.02 → $0.14 (+600%) on the same afternoon its output fell $1.00 → $0.42 (-58.0%). DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 input +168.0% and output +78.5%; V4 Flash 0423 input -50.7% while its output rose +147.3%. Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.5 output $0.238 → $2.00 (+740%), Kimi Latest output $9.34 → $20.00 (+114%). Seven models were retired outright, six of them Mistral batch lines.

Why the two directions in one day is the whole story. A bill is no longer exposed to a market trend, it is exposed to a line. The Terra half is a launch cut that most invoices will not see for a quarter, because it lands on a model ID the customer never checks; the DeepSeek number is a floor being withdrawn from under everyone who moved onto it in writing. Both happened on 27 October, in the same catalogue, and neither vendor emailed anyone.

The sector's own numbers say the same thing. Anthropic's S-1, filed 26 Oct, pairs $16.3bn of half-year revenue with an $80.7bn loss and a ballooning computing bill (Bloomberg, 26 Oct); the Washington Post's read the same day is that there is not enough money in the world to pay for the AI boom (27 Oct). ExtremeTech, this morning: AI has yet to boost profits for most companies. Apollo's chief economist, 24 Oct: no sign of AI payoff outside the tech sector. The cost is landing on the customer's P&L while the benefit is still a slide.

Concretely, same workload, 200M input and 40M output on Terra: at yesterday's rate card ($2 / $12) that is $880/month; at today's ($1 / $5) it is $400; through the batch door ($0.50 / $2.50) it is $200. The margin comes due — 12 rises, 8 cuts on the day Anthropic files its S-1 — 26 Oct 2026

For four months the retail price of frontier AI went one way: down. OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% on 30 Jul to $0.20 / $1.20, cut Sol by 20% on 21 Aug, then halved input and output again with GPT-6 Sol on 22 Sep at $2 / $10. Anthropic made Sonnet 5's launch pricing permanent on 10 Aug and shipped Opus 5.5 on 22 Sep at a total cost reduction of 40-60% against Opus 5. Max Woolf's timeline of both labs, published yesterday, is the clearest record of it: minimaxir.com.

Today the log went the other way: 21 changes, 12 rises, 8 cuts. The single biggest move is DeepSeek V4 Pro 0423, input $0.2088 → $0.7875 (+277.2%) and output at the same rate. Moonshot's Kimi K3 output $10.80 → $20.00 (+85.2%). Qwen3.6 27B output $2.00 → $3.20 (+60.0%). Against that, GLM Latest output fell 68.1% and Qwen3.5-122B dropped 35% on both fields — so the day is not a wall, it is a squeeze in the mid tier.

Why it matters this week rather than next. Anthropic publicly filed its IPO prospectus today: $16.3bn revenue in the six months to June, up 13.5x, a net loss of $80.7bn (mostly a $72.4bn convertible-note charge), and $430bn locked into cloud compute deals. A company that has to show public investors a margin cannot fund a price war out of a private balance sheet. The 80% cuts were market share bought on someone else's money; the S-1 is the moment that money starts asking for a return. Every earlier brief here found the floor falling. This one finds the mid tier being repriced upward on the same day the receipt for the war got filed.

The buying rule that falls out of it: a bill set on a launch price is a bill on a promotional rate, and a discount adopted to win the market expires when the market is won. Anyone who moved workloads onto the cheap tier in July and never re-read the rate is now on a +277% line. Both directions are live in the same week — which is exactly why the only defensible contract is one with a published-rate clause and a re-bid date.
