Why this week

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini on 20 November 2026: a mixture-of-experts model, 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters, NVFP4 weights, a ~20GB memory footprint; the team ran it offline on a MacBook Pro and built a playable flight simulator from it. GGUF weights were on Hugging Face by the next morning and Ollama shipped it within a day. The launch thread took 1,167 points on Hacker News the same day. The log picked the model up at $0.10 input / $0.40 output per million tokens with a 1M context — 30x under Gemini 4 Neon's input price and 37x under its output price.

The same morning the log recorded 26 changes: 16 cuts against 5 rises, including DeepSeek V4.1 Flash down 83.4% to $0.0498 / $0.1992 and Qwen3 Coder 30B down 53.3%. Two separate doors closed on the same bill at once: cheaper tokens, and a competent weight set you can run without renting anything.

What this does not say

  • Not that the models are equal. Thinking Machines reports Inkling 2 Mini ahead of other open-weights models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%) — its own numbers, on its own evaluation setups, which vary by model. The Hugging Face write-up only goes as far as "at least comparable with Qwen 3.8 27B" in vibe tests, and says more validation is needed. A frontier model still wins on the hard 10%.
  • Not that local is free at scale. One laptop serves one or a few users. Concurrency, uptime and context length per request are your problem on your hardware, and nobody is on call at 3am.
  • Not that every workload can move. Anything that needs the best available answer, long-horizon reasoning at frontier level, or vendor guarantees should stay where it is.

What this does say

Most bills are one blended number across a dozen workloads, and the cheap 80% of those workloads — classification, extraction, summarisation, first-draft code, routing — is now served at a fraction of what it was priced at in the budget. That is a routing decision, not a vendor loyalty test, and it is worth $5,000-7,000 a year at the volumes above.
