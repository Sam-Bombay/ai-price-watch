Did your site serve malware through Optimizely?

New: paste a URL and get the answer in seconds, no install and no login — the hosted checker.

On 16 November 2026 malicious JavaScript was injected into the Project JavaScript of Optimizely Web customer projects and served from cdn.optimizely.com — Optimizely's own host, certificate and all — to visitors of 178 organisations. Nothing on the affected sites' own infrastructure was compromised. The compromise sat in the tag layer, where your code review does not look.

The fact most teams get wrong: removing the injected line does not undo what it did. On that day the payload swapped crypto wallet addresses in page text, form fields and the clipboard, skimmed card numbers typed into a site's own forms, and posted page URLs, cookies and screenshots to the attacker. If your snippet was infected, the exposure question is about your visitors, not your servers.

The free check, in one command

The published indicators are specific enough to test for: 1688360.cc, webshell.lol, linkforge.cc, the .icu hosts, and the window._k7 markers. This script fetches a page, finds every third-party tag bundle it loads, fetches those bundles, and searches them. Read-only, no dependencies, no upload.

curl -O https://sam-bombay.github.io/ai-price-watch/tagcheck/check.py
python3 check.py https://yoursite.com https://yoursite.com/pricing https://shop.yoursite.com

It writes a JSON report next to itself and exits 2 if anything needs review. Run it against every template that carries a snippet — the homepage, a product page, a checkout path — not just the root domain.

What it cannot do, said plainly: it reads the page as served now. It cannot reconstruct what was served at 11:20 UTC on 16 November, and it will not catch a loader pointing at a domain that is not on the published list. A clean result today is not a clearance for that day. The only way to answer "were our visitors exposed" is to recover the bundle as served inside the window — from a CDN log, a urlscan capture, an APM sample or a WAF record — and compare it against the archived hashes.

If you are affected, in this order

#Action
1Open every Optimizely project's Project JavaScript and every experiment's custom code. Search for 1688360.cc, atob(, webshell.lol and any script you did not write.
2List who and what has write access — users, API tokens, integrations — and rotate all of it. One snippet was cleaned and then re-infected the same day.
3Treat cards entered into your own form fields on 16 Nov as potentially exposed. Cards inside hosted payment iframes (Stripe, Adyen) were out of reach.
4Treat session tokens and secrets in non-HttpOnly cookies as exposed.
5Deploy a CSP, restrict connect-src, drop 'unsafe-eval', turn on reporting. This is the control that saved two of the affected sites.
